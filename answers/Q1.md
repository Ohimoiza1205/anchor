Pulse is a telemetry ingest service I wrote in Go. In load testing it
held 179,900 concurrent readings from 2,000 simulated devices over 20
seconds on a single vCPU without dropping a message. The design problem
I spent the most time on was backpressure: keeping a bursting stream
from losing data without buffering everything into an unbounded queue
that eventually crashes.

I tested it by opening 2,000 WebSocket connections to my own server,
each sending readings on a timer. It's simulated load, not hardware.

Fort's version is much harder, because devices drop connections mid-set
and Bluetooth fails during a rep. And since Fort can't just reject
overflow the way my service did, every reading has to survive the
failure, because it's training data for a downstream model. That's the
gap that pulled me toward Question 2.
