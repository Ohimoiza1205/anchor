Pulse is a telemetry ingest service I wrote in Go. In its load test it
held 179,900 concurrent readings from 2,000 simulated devices over 20
seconds on a single vCPU without dropping a message. The design problem
I got obsessed with was backpressure: how to keep a bursting stream from
losing data without buffering everything into an unbounded queue that
eventually crashes.

I tested it by opening 2,000 WebSocket connections to my own server,
each sending readings on a timer. That is simulated load, not hardware,
and I say so plainly.

Fort's version of this problem is harder. Devices drop connections
mid-set, Bluetooth fails during a rep, and Fort cannot simply reject
overflow the way my service could, because every reading is training
data for a downstream model. The gap between what I built and what Fort
needs is what pulled me toward this take-home's Question 2 in the first
place.
