What motivates me professionally is building things people use, and the
ambiguity that comes before that, figuring out what to build, not only how
to build it. I like the moment an assumption turns out wrong. The deadlift
rep counter in this project double counting reps is a small example, and
finding that and fixing it is the part of engineering I enjoy most. I want
to work close to both product and engineering, not on one side handing
requirements to the other.

What I want from an internship at Fort is exposure to a stage where those
two things stay coupled: where a technical decision, how to reconstruct a
gap in sensor data, is also a product decision about what a user should
trust. This take-home reflected that directly, which is part of why I
worked through it carefully instead of quickly. Fort's engineers seem to
define problems rather than implement tickets handed to them, and that is
the kind of ownership I am looking for. Sensors, the pipelines that clean
their data, and the product decisions built on top interest me
specifically because a weakness in one layer, a bad interpolation, an
uncalibrated confidence score, changes everything built downstream of it
without announcing itself. I want to work somewhere small enough that I
can see, and be responsible for, that whole chain.
