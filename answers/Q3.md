What motivates me professionally is building things and working through
problems that don't have an obvious answer yet. I like the part where an
assumption turns out wrong, then figuring out why it was wrong. One
example from this project: the deadlift rep counter was double-counting
reps. Catching that and fixing it was more interesting to me than
getting the first version working.

What I want out of an internship at Fort is the chance to work on
problems that sit between engineering and product. I liked this
take-home because a technical question about missing sensor data turned
into a product question about what users should trust. Fort stood out
to me for the same reason, engineers here seem to own both defining
problems and solving them.
